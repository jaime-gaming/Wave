YOUR MUSIC, YOUR DEVICE

Privacy & rights

Understand what stays here, when Wave connects, and the controls you have.

Updated 19 November 2026

Wave is local-first, not a backup service: keep your original music files. Public deployment operators are responsible for their own legal identity and privacy contact.

Your library and playback

Your library stays on your device

Music, metadata, favorites, playlists, images and cached lyrics are kept in this browser's IndexedDB; preferences and the playback session use localStorage. No accounts, analytics, advertising, telemetry, cloud synchronization or audio uploads are implemented. Clearing site data, private browsing or storage eviction can erase the stored copies. Original files on your disk are not changed or deleted.

If you hand Wave a whole folder, the browser's directory handle is stored in the same local database so that folder can be read again; the browser keeps the permission itself and asks for it explicitly. An exported playlist is read on this device; when you choose to import it, song titles and artists are sent to search YouTube for matches. The playlist file and your audio are not uploaded.

Queue, progress and appearance

Your selected song, queue order, repeat/shuffle and playback positions are saved in this browser. Reloading restores a paused session, never automatic playback. This is a current-session bookmark, not an account or a cloud backup. It is scoped to this website origin; a different browser, device or deployment address has separate storage.

Artwork colors are sampled locally to style the interface. Automatic playlist icons combine local cover colors in memory; they do not send images to a design service or modify your audio. Theme and the last palette can be remembered locally so this notice matches Wave.

Local continuous listening

Autoplay selects only from your local songs, using artist, album, available genre/year, favorites, duration and recent-session IDs with weighted variety. No online recommendation profile or streaming audio is requested. Repeat modes take priority; Autoplay can be switched off.

Mix works on the device. Wave analyses a downmixed copy of two songs while they play and plans the handover in memory; nothing is uploaded. During an overlap only the playback changes: the two decks are faded against each other, the low end changes hands, the outgoing song loses its top and the incoming one may be tempo-matched with pitch preserved. All of it is reverted when the overlap ends, and the stored file, its sample rate, its bit depth and its bitrate are never touched.

Native storage and backups

iOS stores selected audio copies, local playlists, history and lyrics in its app container. Uninstalling may remove those copies; keep your originals. The web backup is not a native interchange format. New exports are password-encrypted and signed by a local device key; their size and public key are visible. Older backups are unencrypted. Keep passwords and backups secure and compare the fingerprint independently. Fix Volume is web-only.

Offline copy of the interface

The web edition registers a service worker that stores the interface — HTML, scripts, styles, fonts and icons — so Wave opens without a connection. Audio, artwork, lyrics and video references are never added to that cache; they stay in the library storage described above. Unregistering the service worker or clearing site data removes the offline copy, and the next visit needs a connection.

Song details and artist photos

Song details from a catalogue

When a song is missing an artist, album, year or cover, Wave can fill those gaps from a public catalogue (Apple's iTunes Search API). Only the artist and title already on this device are sent, plus the storefront taken from your browser's language; the cover comes back and is stored here. No audio, no artwork from your files, no account and no listening history. Auto Fill normally skips song-detail lookups for songs whose catalogue details are already complete, and a song you edited by hand is never overwritten. If you separately enable Verify artist and title across the library, Auto Fill also sends names of complete songs to Apple and, if needed, Deezer to check for swapped roles. Other separately enabled searches, such as lyrics or clips, can also send song names. The switches are in Settings → Library and can be turned off at any time.

Choosing Find song details in the editor sends the entered search to Apple and MusicBrainz directly, and to Wave's service for forwarding to Deezer. Those providers receive connection information; no audio, existing artwork, account or listening history is sent. Results are suggestions. Automatic completion may also use Deezer as a fallback. If you separately enable Compare more catalogues in Auto Fill, new automatic searches also send song names to MusicBrainz and Deezer for comparison.

Metadata checks stay on your device

Imports inspect tags, duration and embedded covers locally, using a same-origin worker where supported. Original audio stays unchanged. Local checks do not identify recordings against an external catalogue.

Optional song-detail lookups are off by default. When enabled in Settings, Wave sends the title and artist of incomplete songs to Apple’s iTunes Search API and may download a matching cover. If Apple does not return a match, the query may be forwarded by Wave's service to Deezer. Artist photos may also be requested from Deezer. Those services receive connection information such as your IP address. Audio, existing artwork and listening history are not sent. Turning the option off stops new lookups, but cannot recall a request already sent. Saved details and covers remain local. Local import checks do not use MusicBrainz or Cover Art Archive; an explicit editor search can query MusicBrainz and Deezer as described above. MusicBrainz is also used for new automatic searches only when Compare more catalogues is separately enabled.

Lyrics and word-by-word sources

Online lyrics are optional

Automatic lyrics is off initially. Enabling it in Settings sends song title, artist, optional album/duration and a client identifier to LRCLIB. The provider also receives connection information such as your IP address. Audio and artwork are not sent. The Search online action requests one song even while automatic lookup is off. Turn automatic lookup off to stop new automatic requests; an already sent request cannot be recalled. Saved text remains local and can be removed in the lyric editor. On iOS, the optional automatic search checks only the song being played, and sends its title and artist to LRCLIB. It is off by default.

LRCLIB documentation describes API use. Provider privacy/retention terms and a blanket copyright license for lyrics were not verified during review. API availability does not grant rights to copyrighted text.

Word-by-word lyric sources are optional

Word-by-word highlighting works with the lyrics Wave already has. Two extra community sources that publish word timings — NetEase and Kugou — are asked only if you turn on Word-level lyric sources in Settings, which is off initially. Only the song title and artist are sent; both services see connection information such as your IP address and neither publishes a stable API contract. Their answers are parsed on the device and saved as ordinary lyrics. Turning the switch off stops new requests; lyrics already saved stay available offline.

Videos, playlists and feedback

Public playlist & YouTube links and feedback

When you explicitly search YouTube or paste a Spotify or YouTube URL, the Wave Worker requests bounded public metadata from that provider. No account, audio, local library or listening history is uploaded. Public results can be incomplete; review before saving or use an exported file. Cloudflare processes the request; the provider sees the Worker connection. Wave does not store or cache these lists on its server.

Where implemented, automatic mood detection, harmonic Mix analysis, BS.1770 Fix Volume and Weekly/Monthly/Yearly Remix calculations run 100% locally on your device — never sent to any external server.

The optional opinion prompt can open a prefilled public GitHub issue draft. GitHub receives the feature and the selected response when the draft opens; it becomes public only if you choose to submit it there. No song names or listening history are included. A local cooldown limits how often Wave asks.

Video clips and songs from YouTube

On Android and iOS, an explicitly requested YouTube search sends the search phrase to Wave and YouTube. Opening a video connects the embedded player to YouTube. These native previews are separate from the local library and queue.

Clips. To find the video behind a song, Wave sends only the song title and artist to public YouTube front-ends (Piped and Invidious instances) and keeps the chosen video id in this browser. Those servers also receive connection information such as your IP address. No audio, artwork or library list leaves the device, and a clip is only normally searched for the song you are playing. If you separately enable Find clips across the library and Auto Fill, Wave searches missing clips in the background, one song at a time. Both switches can stop new background requests. Existing clips are not replaced; you can remove a remembered clip from that song's options.

Songs added from YouTube. Wave stores the video id, not a file. The title and length are read from YouTube's oEmbed endpoint; when that request is blocked by the browser, noembed, a Piped instance or an Invidious instance is tried instead, carrying the video id and the usual connection information. Nothing about your library is included.

Playback. A YouTube song, and the muted clip behind Now Playing, stream directly from YouTube inside your browser. That traffic, and the fact that a video was watched, belongs to YouTube's own policy rather than to Wave. Wave does not download, proxy, extract or re-encode that audio, and a song you also imported as a file always plays from your copy instead.

Linking two devices

Linking two devices

Device linking is currently web-only; the Android, Windows and iOS apps cannot link their native libraries. Linking is off until you open Settings → Devices and start it. One device shows a QR code, the other reads it with its camera or opens the same link, and after you confirm on both sides only the songs you ticked are copied, directly from one browser to the other over an encrypted connection. Nothing passes through a Wave server: there is no account, no relay and no automatic library upload, and the files you did not pick never leave the device.

Public Google and Cloudflare STUN services help the two browsers find each other. They see the requesting IP address and port, and it never sees a song. If the two networks refuse a direct connection the link fails and says so. Linked devices, and a borrowed song you keep, are stored on the device until you remove them; the songs you keep are ordinary local copies and are never deleted behind your back.

The camera asks for permission only when the scan button is pressed and closes as soon as a code is read or the panel is dismissed. The picture is examined in the page and no frame is stored or sent anywhere. A device with no camera can open the pairing link instead.

Your controls

Your controls

  • Keep your originals. Browser storage can be cleared or evicted. Wave is not a backup.
  • Remove one song or playlist. Use its options in the library. Removing a playlist does not delete the music.
  • Remove downloaded lyrics. Open the lyric editor. Manual lyrics take precedence over online matches.
  • Clear everything for this site. Use your browser’s site-data controls. This removes local copies, artwork, preferences and saved progress; it cannot erase the original files on disk or copies held elsewhere.

Rights, hosting and contact

The website host

The production player is served by Cloudflare Workers and static assets. Cloudflare may process IP/device/request information under its privacy policy. GitHub hosts source code and native release downloads, not the production player. Fonts are bundled with Wave.

Music, lyrics and artwork rights

Use only content you are entitled to access and use. Free/open-source software does not authorize copying or distributing music, lyrics or covers. Wave bundles no music catalogue, bypasses no access controls and does not download streaming audio. Spain's private-copy exception has specific cumulative conditions; it is not permission to download arbitrary online material. This is not legal advice or certification.

Contact and further information

Read the technical privacy notice, and project source. Public issues are visible to others: do not attach personal music, private metadata or credentials. Applicable privacy rights and complaint channels must be explained by the actual deployment operator.
